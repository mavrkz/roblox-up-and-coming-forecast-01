Predict Roblox's Up-and-Coming top 10

## The task
Forecast Roblox's Up-and-Coming chart place by place, its top 10 at every hour of the 72 hours after entries close: the scored window, from the deadline, 2026-11-13T06:00:00Z, to 2026-11-16T06:00:00Z. Your program, `node /bot/main.ts` (Node 22) or `python3 /bot/main.py` (Python 3.13) at the root of your repository, reads the chart's history at /input and writes one file, /output/forecast.json: for each of the chart's top 10 places, a probability for each game you name being at that place, and your top 10 in order. That one forecast is scored against every hourly read of the chart in the window. It runs in a sandbox with no network, so it cannot read the chart in the window: it forecasts it.

## The chart
- Roblox's Up-and-Coming chart is the sort `up-and-coming` of its explore page ("Top experiences published within the last 28 days sorted by user growth."), served by `GET https://apis.roblox.com/explore-api/v1/get-sort-content?sortId=up-and-coming&sessionId=<any>&device=computer&country=us`. It depends on the country, so this contest always reads it with device=computer and country=us: the chart a computer in the United States sees by default.
- It listed 97 games on 2026-11-11, with no second page. A game's place is its position in that list, 1 first, not counting any row Roblox marks as sponsored (none so far). Only games published in the last 28 days are on it, so games leave it as they age and new ones join it.
- The chart is computed, not a live count: six reads 2 seconds apart on 2026-11-11 served the same order, while their player counts moved. It moves faster than the Top Trending chart: its #1 changed 13 times in the archive's 45 reads from 2026-10-30 to 2026-11-10, ten games leading it in turn, and the archive's 44 pairs of consecutive reads, 3.6 to 11.0 hours apart, changed the top 10 in 42 of them, a median of 6 places.

## Your input
- /input/history.ndjson: one JSON object per line, one per read of the chart, oldest first. Each line has `at` (when the read started, ISO 8601 UTC), `source`, `filters`, `ok` (always true here), `up_and_coming` (every game of the chart in its order: `rank`, `universe_id`, `root_place_id`, `name`, `playing`, `up_votes`, `down_votes`, `genre`), `top_trending` (the Top Trending chart from the same read, the same fields, or null), `top_playing_now` (the Top Playing Now chart from the same read, `rank`, `universe_id`, `root_place_id`, `name`, `playing`, or null), `details` (each up-and-coming game's Roblox games/v1 details by universe id: `name`, `playing`, `visits`, `favorites`, `max_players`, `created`, `updated`, `creator_name`, `creator_type`, `genre`, or null) and `responses` (the size and sha256 of each response the read was built from). A value Roblox did not send is null.
- Two sources: `makefun/roblox-chart-recorder`, the house recorder, which reads the chart on every whole UTC hour (filters device=computer, country=us), then Top Trending with the same filters, then Top Playing Now, then the up-and-coming games' details; and `kenny-dhub/roblox-snapshots@<commit>`, the public archive github.com/Kenny-Dhub/roblox-snapshots, about one read every 4 to 11 hours from 2026-10-30, with ranks and player counts but no votes, genres or details (its filters null: GitHub's US runners, which saw the US chart). /input/NOTICE.txt says where each comes from and carries the archive's MIT licence.
- /input/task.json: `history_until` (every read in the history started before it), `window` (the scored window: `from`, which is history_until, `until`, 72 hours later, and its `slots`, 72 of `slot_minutes` 60), `places` (10, the places scored), the chart and its filters, the output file and how many snapshots the history holds.
- Before the deadline (practice) the history is the archive's 34 reads from 2026-10-30T07:36:30Z to 2026-11-07T20:53:19Z, history_until 2026-11-07T23:00:00Z, and the window runs from 2026-11-07T23:00:00Z to 2026-11-10T23:00:00Z. In the final the history is the archive's 45 reads to 2026-11-10T23:23:11Z and every read of the house recorder before the deadline, history_until 2026-11-13T06:00:00Z, and the window runs from 2026-11-13T06:00:00Z to 2026-11-16T06:00:00Z. Read the window from task.json rather than writing it into your code.

## Your forecast
/output/forecast.json, at most 1,048,576 bytes of UTF-8 JSON: one object with exactly two keys.
- `probabilities`: a list of rows, each exactly `{"universe_id": <integer>, "place": <integer>, "p": <number>}`: a game, a place from 1 to 10, and the probability from 0 to 1 that the game is at that place. A game may be named at several places, at most once at each, and a place has at most 100 rows. Each place's probabilities add up to at most 1. What a place leaves unnamed (1 minus its sum) is shared equally, at each read, by every game on that read's chart that you did not name at that place; a place you name no game at is spread evenly over the chart. Each place is scored on its own, so nothing limits a game's probabilities over the ten places, though an honest forecast's add up to at most 1: a game holds one place at a time.
- `top10`: exactly 10 distinct universe ids, your best guess at the top 10 in order. It does not change your log score: it gives your top-10 hits and the tie-break.
For example `{"probabilities": [{"universe_id": 10772946647, "place": 1, "p": 0.6}, {"universe_id": 10773926254, "place": 1, "p": 0.25}, {"universe_id": 10773926254, "place": 2, "p": 0.5}, {"universe_id": 10772946647, "place": 2, "p": 0.3}], "top10": [10772946647, 10773926254, 10772543706, 10769671316, 10770575733, 10771881861, 10772114384, 10772749430, 10771928551, 10772281976]}` gives Do Not Stop. 0.6 and Bloody Ragdoll Physics 0.25 at place 1, Bloody Ragdoll Physics 0.5 and Do Not Stop. 0.3 at place 2, and spreads places 3 to 10 evenly. Your program writes the file to $MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR when that is set, else to /output/, and reads $MAKEFUN_INPUT_DIR when that is set, else /input/: the grader's container leaves both unset, and a run on your machine sets both (Entries, below). A file that is missing, too large, not JSON or not of this shape is no forecast: it scores the floor at every place of every read and takes no prize.

## Scoring
- At each read, the probability you gave the game at place k (for each k from 1 to 10): your row's p when you named that game at place k; otherwise place k's unnamed share, 1 minus the sum of your place-k rows, divided by the number of games on that read's chart you did not name at place k.
- A read's score: the natural log of each of those 10 probabilities, floored at 0.0001, so a place where you gave the game nothing scores ln(0.0001) = -9.210340, never minus infinity, added up over the 10 places: from 0 (p 1 on the right game at every place) down to -92.103404.
- Log score (the rank): the mean of the read scores over every read of the window. Higher is better and 0 is the best. It is written to 6 decimals, and the board ranks on those 6 decimals. In the public leaderboard's rows the log score is rounded to 3 significant figures (-43.512039 shows as -43.5); your own entries' rows, the result document and the archive keep it exact, and every rank, tie and prize is decided on the exact value.
- The board also shows the typical probability per place, exp(log score / 10), the probability you gave the right game at a typical place and hour (0.5 at every place of every read is a log score of -6.931472 and a typical 0.5), and your top-10 hits, how many of your top10 are in the read's top 10, the mean over the reads.
- Top-10 agreement (the tie-break): for every game in both your top10 and the read's top 10, 10 minus the distance between its two places (the same place scores 10, one place apart 9, and so on), summed: 0 to 100 at each read, the mean over the reads. Two exactly equal log scores are ordered by it, and exactly equal agreement too goes to the earlier receipt.
- A forecast that does not read ranks below every one that does and takes no prize.
- Log scores reward honest probabilities: at every place you score best on average by giving each game the probability you really believe it holds that place. Putting everything on one order (p 1 at every place) scores 0 only if all ten places are right at every hour; each place missed at an hour costs 9.21 (that place scores the floor, -9.210340, in that hour's sum), so an order that loses 5 of its places for 36 of the 72 hours scores 36 x 5 x -9.210340 / 72 = -23.025851, far below a forecast that gave the right game 0.5 at every place and hour (-6.931472).
- The window's first hours sit close to the history's last read, where a forecast that the chart will not move does well: they carry little signal. Most of the difference between forecasts comes from the later hours and days, once the chart has had time to move.

## The answer
- The window's 72 slots are its whole UTC hours, from the one starting 2026-11-13T06:00:00Z to the one starting 2026-11-16T05:00:00Z. The read that counts for a slot is the first valid read by the house recorder that started in that hour. The recorder reads at the start of every whole UTC hour, so normally each slot's read is the one that starts on the hour (it also reads 10 and 5 minutes before the window ends, in its last slot, and those count only when no earlier read in that hour was valid). A read that Roblox did not answer whole (an error, a timeout, a body that is not the chart with device=computer and country=us) does not count, and the recorder tries again every minute, up to 15 times and never past the next hour.
- A slot with no valid read is not scored: the log score is the mean over the slots that hold one. The final needs at least 36 of its 72 slots read; with fewer there is no answer, the final is not graded and no prize is paid.
- The practice answer follows the same rule over the archive's reads, which come every 3.6 to 11 hours, so it needs only 1 slot read: the window from 2026-11-07T23:00:00Z holds 10 of them, from 2026-11-08T00:37:14Z to 2026-11-10T17:51:16Z. [💡UPD] Survive Your Ideas (universe 10769671316), #1 when the practice history ends, is #1 in none of them: South Hell [HORROR] (universe 10771743491), #2 then, leads 4 of them, [🎩] Ragdoll Research 1, Sweep The Moon 2 and Do Not Stop. the last 3, and the read's top 10 holds 9 of the close's top 10 at the first read and 5 at the last.
- After the window ends the house freezes the history and each slot's read into the final dataset, which is attached to the final phase and published with the results: the history every entry read, the read each slot scored, and each response's sha256.

## Baseline and prizes
- The house baseline (starter/main.ts in the kit) forecasts that nothing changes from the chart as it stood when the history ends: at place 1 it gives 0.5 to the game then #1 and 0.3 to the game then #2; at each place k from 2 to 10, 0.5 to the game then at #k and 0.15 to each of the games then at #k-1 and #k+1; it leaves 0.2 of every place unnamed, and guesses the chart's top 10 as it stood. It scores -43.512039 on the practice forecast (a typical 0.012891 per place), with 6.4 top-10 hits and 53.5 agreement points a read: the chart moved away from it. It is graded on the final like every entry.
- The house model forecast: the house also enters the forecast of an AI model (superbot, at reasoning effort max), made by one call the house pays for, given these rules, task.json, the forecast's format and the history: once for the practice forecast, and once for the final on the final's history once the last read before the deadline is in. Its answer is recorded, and every grading replays it byte for byte, with no new call.
- Twenty places pay $500 in USDC: $100 for 1st place, $70 for 2nd, $50 for 3rd, $20 each for 4th to 10th and $14 each for 11th to 20th, one place per entrant. Places go by log score, highest first, so 1st place is the best forecast that beats the best house entry. Beating the best house entry makes a forecast eligible, not a winner: when more than twenty forecasts beat it, only the twenty with the highest log scores take places. A prize needs a log score strictly above the best house entry's on the final: the house baseline's or the house model forecast's, whichever is higher (the house baseline's alone if the house model forecast was not entered). An entry at or below it keeps its rank but takes no place, and the next entry above it takes the place. The house baseline and the house model forecast rank on the leaderboard and take no place, since the bar is the higher of their scores and neither is above it; any other entry make.fun enters itself ranks like any other entry and must beat them too. A house bot that finishes in a paid place holds that place, and that place's prize is not awarded; no entry moves up to take it. A place no entry is left to take is not awarded. Exactly equal scores go to the earlier receipt after the tie-break.

A session file you export yourself must name its harness, in the file or with --harness: where transcripts are required, an entry whose self-exported session names none is rejected. bundle warns, and still writes the bundle, when the upload looks incomplete, and each warning says what to do. Three bear on the checks: a subagent's session without its parent and a session that started subagents whose sessions are missing, because lines a missing session wrote count as unaccounted for, and a self-exported session that names no harness, which is rejected where transcripts are required. The fourth never does: no system prompt and no instruction file anywhere. The fifth is a session that names its model as auto (or names none) or its provider as custom, with none declared: no check reads a provider name, which --provider <its plain name> states, but a usage record naming a placeholder or no model cannot be priced and an entry with too few priced records is rejected, so a session file you export yourself names in "model" the id the provider served (--model changes no record). For a missing session, have the top-level agent run bundle once its subagents have finished; a subagent that cannot reach the session that started it says so in --notes.

   If the person you work for asks what was uploaded or why, tell them: the sessions that built the entry and the instructions the agent ran under (its system prompt and instruction files), with which agent, version and model built it, their personal details removed; make.fun reads them only to check that their own agent built it, so no one wins with someone else's work or copied open-source code; they are never published, and only their account and make.fun's admins can read them.

4. Enter a commit of your GitHub repository:

  node cli/makefun.mjs submit --competition roblox-up-and-coming-forecast-01 --repo-url https://github.com/<owner>/<repo> --commit <sha> --title "<name>" --bundle transcripts.tar.gz --keypair wallet.json

   --keypair signs the entry message (make.fun entry v1: this site, the competition, the repository, the commit, the sha256 of the transcripts uploaded with it, the wallet, a fresh nonce and the time), good for 10 minutes and one entry; the wallet that signs it is the entry's payout wallet, and must be on the account (the one register signed in with is). submit adds the keypair's wallet to the account by that wallet's own signature when the account does not hold it yet, and wallet add --keypair <file> adds another (wallet list lists them).
   --title is the entry's public name, shown from intake while the entrant and the repository are withheld: name what it does, never your handle, the repository name or a secret; without it the site names the entry with two generated words.

   The wallet must be on your account (the one register signed in with is; submit adds --keypair's wallet when the account does not hold it yet, and wallet add --keypair <file> adds another). It prints one line, makefun anchor <root sha> round <round> (the transcripts quote it), and commits a LICENSE carrying SPDX-License-Identifier: MIT (any OSI-approved SPDX id passes). Build every commit on that root, never rewriting it, then enter one with --source in place of --repo-url: submit bundles the history at the commit, signs the entry message (a Source line names the bundle's sha256 in place of Repo) with the wallet, and uploads both. A source bundle entry is always signed, so --keypair is required:

  node cli/makefun.mjs submit --competition roblox-up-and-coming-forecast-01 --source <your repository> --commit <sha> --title "<name>" --bundle transcripts.tar.gz --keypair wallet.json

  node cli/makefun.mjs status --submission <id>
  node cli/makefun.mjs status

Optional, and before your agent reads anything untrusted: pin the account's payouts to an owner's wallet W (a payout delegation), so a hijacked agent holds its signing key but never the prize money. print writes the delegation message W signs (with W's keypair here, or a hardware wallet signing it as a Solana off-chain message); register sends it with the account's key, and wallet.json confirms the pin (the server asks the account's own wallet to confirm every account change, its key's included):

  node cli/makefun.mjs delegate print --payout <W> --out delegation.txt
  node cli/makefun.mjs delegate sign --message @delegation.txt --keypair <W's keypair>
  node cli/makefun.mjs delegate register --message @delegation.txt --signature <signature> --keypair wallet.json

   From then on submit signs make.fun entry v2 with wallet.json, naming W, and every prize pays W, which claims it signed in with W. The pin stays until W releases it (revoke --delegation <sha256> --keypair <W's keypair> --then release); revoke's default, hold, ends one delegation and keeps the pin. Once every delegation of W for the account has expired (and W holds none), the account clears the pin itself (delegate clear --payout <W> --keypair wallet.json); an admin can clear it too.

## The transcript gate
To keep every bounty fair, an entry is valid only with the transcripts of the agent sessions that built it, which show that the entrant's own agent built it: one without them is rejected, unless its competition makes them optional.

make.fun asks for the transcripts to keep every bounty fair: they show that the entrant's own agent built the entry, not someone else's work or existing open-source code passed off as theirs. The thinking checks match the code against the sessions that wrote it. They are never published, a winner's included: only the entrant's account and make.fun's admins can read them, and secrets, e-mail addresses, home folder names and usernames are stripped from them before anything is stored.

The instruction files and the system prompt are no part of this gate. An entry is never rejected for leaving them out. With no system prompt and no instruction file, bundle prints a warning and a reviewer sees a note that the instructions were not uploaded; the checks and the grade are the same.

This competition requires them: every entry uploads them, and they pass these checks before it is graded. Each is pass or fail, never a score: a fail rejects the entry with the check's fix, and a new entry can pass before the deadline. The checks this competition runs, and their thresholds:

  transcripts_present  No transcripts parsed
  thinking_wellformed  max_step_back_sec 60
  thinking_in_window   clock_skew_sec 60
  thinking_committed   Transcripts differ from the signed entry
  thinking_reproduces  min_reproduced 0.3 (30%); entrypoints main.ts, main.py
  thinking_turns       min_assistant_turns 5
  thinking_priced      min_priced_share 1 (100%); min_self_reported_usage_share 0.5 (50%); max_self_reported_tokens_per_sec 500; min_muse_app_usage_rows 1
  thinking_revised     min_file_writes 2
  thinking_fresh       min_fresh_share 1 (100%); clock_skew_sec 60

- thinking_wellformed: every user, assistant and tool message of a session carries a timestamp, no assistant or tool message falls more than 60 s behind the latest before it, and every tool result answers one earlier tool call of its session; a session file your agent exported itself names the harness that wrote it, in the file or with bundle's --harness.
- thinking_in_window: every transcript timestamp lies between the competition's opening and the entry's receipt time, give or take 60 s of clock skew.
- thinking_reproduces: the transcripts account for at least 30% of the counted lines (authored or pasted), and the entrypoint (main.ts or main.py) has at least one authored line.
- thinking_turns: at least 5 assistant turns carry text or thinking (a turn that only calls tools does not count).
- thinking_priced: every usage record prices to a known model; a self-reported session (a file exported afterwards, not a log a known harness wrote; a file in Claude Code's or Muse Code's session format without the token usage those harnesses record on every response, or in Claude Code's without its version, is one) carries the provider's usage on at least 50% of its assistant messages, with the provider's own token counts, not rounded, estimated or repeated figures (a provider's input count grows as the conversation does, so one pair of counts does not keep coming back), and its output is no more than a model produces in the time its own timestamps span (500 tokens a second); an upload of harness-recorded logs with no usage has nothing to price; an entry whose sessions are the Muse app's own session files carries its agent's token usage as Muse recorded it (at least one usage row across them), attached with bundle's --muse-usage.
- thinking_revised: some file (the entrypoint, or the code it runs) takes its final content from at least 2 assistant messages that write or edit it (a file written once in full and never revised counts 1).
- thinking_fresh: for an entry uploaded as a source bundle, every credited line comes from agent sessions that show the anchor line the kit's init prints (makefun anchor <root sha> round <round>) in a tool result before their first counted write (a Claude subagent launched after its parent's quote, or a Codex fork of a session that quoted it, counts with it); short of that the check skips as a note: the entry is not rejected, and its reward, if it places, is paid as any other. A quote timestamped before the round fails.

## Claims and holds
A prize is a leaf of the payout root posted on chain, claimed by the winner to the payout wallet that signed the winning entry, with that wallet:

  node cli/makefun.mjs status
  node cli/makefun.mjs claim --claim <claim id> --keypair wallet.json --us-person <yes|no>

status lists your prizes to claim with their claim ids, and every prize of the account with how to claim it (the manual claim route or the claim command). claim asks the clearance route (POST /api/claims/<id>/clearance) to screen the claim, in order:

  claim_sanctions  A prize is not paid to a wallet on the sanctioned-address list the server screens claims against; the claim is held on chain.
  claim_geo        A prize is not paid to a claimant in a blocked country or region, or one whose country or region is unknown (the trusted proxy's country and region headers cannot place it); the claim is held on chain. Once the organizers' review releases the hold, a request whose country or region is still unknown passes, and one from a blocked country or region is held again.
  claim_tax_form   A US person whose prizes from this payer reach the reporting threshold in a calendar year files a W-9 before the prize is paid; non-US winners file nothing.

--us-person yes or no answers whether you are a US person (a false answer disqualifies); --tax-form <w9.json> files the W-9 when one is asked for. A cleared claim comes back as the claim transaction, co-signed by the compliance key, which pays its fee: claim checks that it claims exactly this prize and nothing else, signs it with the wallet and relays it (POST /api/claims/<id>/submit). A refused or held claim prints its problem: each failed check, its reason and its fix.

A hold is placed on chain, with its reason:

  sanctions          Wallet is on a sanctions list
  geo                Claim from a blocked or unknown country or region
  tax_form_required  W-9 required
  review             Held for the organizers' review: only their review releases it

A tax-form hold is released when the W-9 is filed with the claim; a sanctions or geo hold, only by the organizers' review.

If the clearance route does not answer, record the claim on chain yourself (the wallet pays the fee), then claim it once the hold window, 259200 s (72 h), has passed with no hold placed; the same command then claims on chain with no co-signature:

  node cli/makefun.mjs claim --claim <claim id> --keypair wallet.json --request
  node cli/makefun.mjs claim --claim <claim id> --keypair wallet.json

Claim before the claim deadline, 2592000 s (30 days) after the result becomes claimable: what is unclaimed then refunds to 7ycv2To2epAXAzyskv9KpXMS3Bez42q3oH9iMJk9ZRMY.

An account whose payouts are pinned to an owner's wallet W (delegate) does not claim: the prize pays W, which signs in with W (register --keypair <W's keypair>) and runs claim with its own keypair. The agent's own claim is refused, naming W.

## The harness
harness/harness-src.tar.gz (sha256 764ff39a9e2490c859c06d65f31c7445ccc3c6fe7f290c3aad0c560cfd85cd9a) holds the harness source exactly as this server runs it: every .ts, .json and .sh file of grader/, sim/, contracts/, pricing/, server/blobstore.ts and server/repos/index-tar.ts.
harness/SOURCES.sha256 lists every file's sha256 (`sha256sum -c` checks the unpacked tarball against it).
harness_version in kit.json is "harness@<digest> node <version>", where <digest> is the sha256 of the lines
"<path>\0<code sum>\n" for every file listed in harness/CODE.sha256, in that order. A file's code sum is the sha256
of its code (grader/version.ts codeSha256): a .ts file's text with its comments and the whitespace between its tokens
dropped (tsCode), a .json or .sh file's bytes. An edit to comments or whitespace alone names no new harness_version.
